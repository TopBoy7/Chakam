================================================================================
ABSTRACT
================================================================================

This report presents the design, architecture, and implementation of Chakam,
a smart classroom management system that integrates real-time occupancy
detection with biometric face recognition to automate student attendance
tracking. The system addresses the limitations of traditional manual attendance
methods by leveraging computer vision — specifically the YOLOv8 object detection
model and the dlib-based face recognition pipeline — to provide continuous,
unobtrusive, and accurate attendance marking during academic sessions.

The backend is built on FastAPI, an asynchronous Python web framework, and
communicates with a MongoDB Atlas database. The frontend is developed using
React 18 and TypeScript, receiving live updates through a persistent WebSocket
connection. The system also incorporates full compliance with the Nigeria Data
Protection Act (NDPA) 2023, implementing the right to erasure, the right of
access, and strict data minimisation principles — storing only mathematical
face embeddings rather than photographs.

Results demonstrate that the system can identify enrolled students from live
camera frames, update attendance records in real time, and provide lecturers
with an accurate session log without requiring any manual intervention during
the class.

CHAPTER ONE: INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

1.1 Background to the Study
----------------------------
The conventional approach to recording student attendance in Nigerian higher
education institutions involves lecturers or class representatives manually
calling names, circulating paper sheets, or using basic digital spreadsheets.
These methods are time-consuming, prone to proxy attendance (where one student
signs in on behalf of another), and produce records that are difficult to
aggregate, analyse, or audit at scale. As class sizes grow and the administrative
burden on academic staff increases, there is a compelling need for an automated,
reliable, and legally compliant alternative.

Concurrent to attendance challenges, physical space management in educational
institutions suffers from poor visibility. Administrators have no real-time
means of knowing how many students occupy a given room at any moment, making
it difficult to optimise room allocation, enforce fire safety capacity limits,
or respond to emergencies.

Advances in computer vision, deep learning, and edge computing have made it
feasible to address both problems simultaneously using a single camera device
per classroom. Object detection models such as YOLO (You Only Look Once) can
count people in a frame with high accuracy and low latency. Face recognition
libraries built on top of deep residual networks can identify specific
individuals from a database of known faces with sufficient accuracy for
controlled environments such as classrooms.

Chakam was designed to unify both capabilities — occupancy monitoring and
biometric attendance — in a single, privacy-aware, institutionally deployable
platform.


1.2 Statement of the Problem
------------------------------
The following specific problems motivated this project:

  i.   Manual attendance recording is susceptible to proxy fraud, transcription
       errors, and deliberate manipulation by students.

  ii.  Attendance data collected on paper must be manually transferred to
       digital records, creating duplication of effort and introducing further
       error.

  iii. There is no mechanism in most institutions for students to independently
       verify their attendance records in real time, leading to disputes at the
       end of the semester.

  iv.  Classroom space utilisation data is unavailable, preventing informed
       timetabling and room allocation decisions.

  v.   Existing biometric attendance solutions, particularly fingerprint-based
       systems, require physical contact, are slow for large classes, and are
       not compliant with current data protection principles around data
       minimisation.


1.3 Aim and Objectives
------------------------
The aim of this project is to design and implement a smart classroom system
that automates both occupancy monitoring and student attendance tracking using
computer vision, with full compliance with the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023.

The specific objectives are:

  i.   To develop a backend API capable of receiving images from classroom
       devices and performing real-time person detection using the YOLOv8 model.

  ii.  To implement a face recognition pipeline that identifies enrolled students
       from live frames and marks their attendance automatically.

  iii. To build a student self-registration portal through which students can
       upload their face photographs and give explicit biometric consent prior
       to being enrolled in the system.

  iv.  To provide lecturers with a real-time dashboard that reflects live
       attendance and allows manual correction where necessary.

  v.   To implement consent management features as required by NDPA 2023,
       including the right to erasure of biometric data and the right of access
       to all stored personal data.

  vi.  To expose a public student-facing portal where any student can look up
       their own attendance record without requiring an account or login.


1.4 Scope of the Study
------------------------
This project covers the server-side API, the web-based frontend interface, and
the integration between occupancy detection and attendance tracking. The design
of the physical camera device and its firmware is outside the scope of this
report, though the API endpoints it communicates with are fully specified and
implemented. The system is designed and tested for the local network and cloud
deployment environments and does not include a native mobile application.


1.5 Significance of the Study
-------------------------------
This system demonstrates that biometric attendance marking can be implemented
in a privacy-first manner — without retaining photographs — while remaining
accurate and operationally practical. It provides a reference architecture for
institutions seeking to automate attendance in compliance with the NDPA. The
dual-purpose nature of the image endpoint (occupancy and attendance from the
same frame) also offers an efficient model for deploying computer vision in
resource-constrained institutional settings.


================================================================================
CHAPTER TWO: LITERATURE REVIEW
================================================================================

2.1 Attendance Management Systems
-----------------------------------
Traditional attendance management has been widely studied. Baig et al. (2019)
reviewed manual, RFID, and fingerprint-based systems, concluding that manual
systems remain predominant in developing countries despite their well-documented
weaknesses, primarily due to cost and infrastructure constraints. Fingerprint
systems, while effective for small groups, suffer from latency issues in large
lecture settings where hundreds of students must interact with a single reader.

Camera-based attendance systems have attracted increasing research interest.
Guo et al. (2020) demonstrated a multi-face recognition system capable of
identifying students from a single wide-angle classroom photograph. However,
their system required high image resolution and a controlled lighting setup.
The approach adopted in this project uses dlib's pre-trained ResNet face
encoder, which is more robust to lighting variation and does not require
purpose-built hardware beyond a standard USB or IP camera.


2.2 Object Detection with YOLO
--------------------------------
The YOLO (You Only Look Once) architecture, first introduced by Redmon et al.
(2016), unified object detection into a single neural network pass, enabling
real-time inference. Subsequent versions have progressively improved accuracy
and efficiency. YOLOv8, released by Ultralytics in 2023, introduced a new
anchor-free detection head and improved backbone architecture. The nano variant
(yolov8n.pt) used in this project is optimised for inference speed at the cost
of some accuracy, making it suitable for embedded or server-side deployment
where low latency is required.

In the context of occupancy detection, Shen et al. (2021) demonstrated that
YOLO-based person counting achieves competitive accuracy compared to purpose-
built crowd counting models while offering broader deployment flexibility. This
informed the decision to use YOLOv8 as the occupancy detection backbone rather
than a dedicated regression-based density estimation model.


2.3 Face Recognition
---------------------
The face recognition library used in this project wraps dlib's face recognition
model, based on the work of King (2015) and a ResNet architecture trained on
a large face dataset. The model produces a 128-dimensional face embedding
vector for any given face image. Recognition is performed by computing the
Euclidean distance between the embedding of an unknown face and the embeddings
of known individuals. A threshold of 0.6 is typically recommended by the
library authors; this project uses 0.55 for a more conservative match rate
suitable for a high-stakes academic context.

Compared to approaches based on Principal Component Analysis (PCA/Eigenfaces)
or Local Binary Pattern Histograms (LBPH), deep embedding-based methods offer
substantially better robustness to illumination changes, pose variation, and
aging — all of which are practically relevant in a university classroom setting.


2.4 Data Protection in Biometric Systems
------------------------------------------
The ethical and legal dimensions of biometric data collection in educational
settings have received increasing attention. The Nigeria Data Protection Act
2023 establishes a legal framework that imposes explicit requirements on the
collection, storage, and erasure of personal data, including biometric
information. Section 24 mandates data minimisation — collecting only what is
strictly necessary for the stated purpose. Section 36 establishes the right to
erasure, compelling data controllers to permanently delete personal data on
request.

Chakam implements both requirements architecturally: photographs are discarded
immediately after embedding extraction (data minimisation), and a dedicated
endpoint permanently clears all stored embeddings while preserving non-biometric
records (right to erasure).

CHAPTER THREE: SYSTEM DESIGN AND ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

3.1 System Overview
---------------------
Chakam consists of four principal components that interact across two network
layers:

  1. Physical camera devices deployed in classrooms, which capture images and
     POST them to the backend at configurable intervals.

  2. A FastAPI backend server that processes images, manages the database, and
     serves the REST API consumed by both devices and the frontend.

  3. A MongoDB Atlas cloud database that persists all application data across
     six collections.

  4. A React/TypeScript frontend that provides the administrative and student-
     facing interfaces and receives real-time updates via WebSocket.

Figure 3.1 illustrates the high-level architecture:

  [Camera Device] ──POST image──► [FastAPI Backend]
                                        │
                       ┌────────────────┤
                       ▼                ▼
                   [MongoDB]      [Cloudinary]
                       │
                       ├──WebSocket broadcast──► [Admin Dashboard]
                       └──REST API──────────────► [Frontend Pages]


3.2 Backend Architecture
--------------------------
The backend is implemented in Python 3.11 using FastAPI, an ASGI-compliant
web framework built on Starlette and Pydantic. FastAPI was selected for its
native support for async/await, automatic OpenAPI documentation generation,
and tight integration with Pydantic for request and response validation.

All I/O-bound operations — database reads and writes, cloud storage uploads,
and WebSocket broadcasts — are implemented as async coroutines using Python's
asyncio event loop. CPU-bound operations — specifically YOLO inference and
face encoding — are offloaded to a thread pool executor via
asyncio.get_running_loop().run_in_executor() to avoid blocking the event loop
during computationally intensive tasks.

The server is served by Uvicorn, a high-performance ASGI server, and is
configured to run on port 8000. Cross-Origin Resource Sharing (CORS) is
enabled for all origins to facilitate development and institutional deployment
across different domains.

A global exception handler is registered on the FastAPI application to ensure
that all unhandled exceptions return a JSON response with the appropriate CORS
headers. Without this, unhandled 500 errors propagate past the CORS middleware
before headers can be injected, causing the browser to display an opaque
network error rather than the actual failure message.


3.3 Database Design
--------------------
MongoDB Atlas is used as the database engine. MongoDB was selected over a
relational database for its document-oriented model, which naturally represents
the nested structure of session and attendance data — a Session document
contains an embedded array of AttendanceEntry subdocuments, eliminating the
need for a JOIN between sessions and attendance tables.

The database is named smartclassDB and contains six collections:

  classrooms   — One document per physical classroom, updated in real time
                 by the YOLO occupancy pipeline.

  lecturers    — One document per registered lecturer.

  courses      — One document per academic course, including the UUID
                 registration token used for student self-registration.

  students     — One document per student, containing all face embedding
                 vectors (up to five per student) and full NDPA consent
                 metadata.

  enrollments  — Junction collection linking a student (by matricNumber) to
                 a course (by courseCode). One document per student-course pair.

  sessions     — One document per class session, with an embedded array of
                 AttendanceEntry subdocuments that grows as students are
                 identified by the face recognition pipeline.

All database operations are implemented as async functions in database.py using
Motor, the async MongoDB driver for Python. Queries are designed to be
idempotent where possible — particularly the attendance marking function, which
checks for existing records before inserting to prevent duplicates.


3.4 Frontend Architecture
---------------------------
The frontend is a single-page application (SPA) built with React 18, TypeScript,
and Vite. The UI component library is ShadCN UI, which provides pre-built,
accessible components based on Radix UI primitives and styled with Tailwind CSS.

All HTTP communication is centralised in a single api object defined in
src/lib/api.ts. No component issues fetch calls directly. This architectural
decision ensures that the base URL, error parsing, and data transformation are
maintained in a single location, reducing duplication and simplifying future
changes to the API contract.

Data transformation functions (transformCourse, transformSession,
transformLecturer, etc.) normalise raw MongoDB document structures into typed
TypeScript interfaces before they reach component state, ensuring that
components always work with consistently shaped data regardless of backend
changes.

Authentication is implemented as a client-side check using localStorage. The
admin password is stored in the VITE_ADMIN_PASSWORD environment variable and
baked into the frontend bundle at build time. This approach is suitable for
a trusted internal network environment but would need to be replaced with
server-side JWT or OAuth for a publicly accessible production system.

Real-time updates are managed by a custom React hook
(useClassroomWebSocket.ts) that maintains a persistent WebSocket connection
to the backend. The hook implements exponential backoff reconnection — starting
at one second and doubling up to a maximum of thirty seconds — and sends a
keep-alive ping every twenty-five seconds to prevent idle timeout disconnects
from intermediary proxies.


3.5 Environment Configuration
-------------------------------
The backend reads its configuration from a .env file validated by env.py at
startup. If any required variable is absent, the server exits immediately with
an error message rather than running in an undefined state. Required variables
are:

  DATABASE_URL          — MongoDB Atlas connection string (TLS-enabled)
  CLOUDINARY_API_KEY    — Cloudinary API credentials for image storage
  CLOUDINARY_API_SECRET
  CLOUDINARY_CLOUD_NAME

The frontend reads its configuration from a separate .env file. Variables are
prefixed with VITE_ and are inlined into the JavaScript bundle at build time:

  VITE_API_BASE_URL   — Backend HTTP base URL
  VITE_WS_URL         — Backend WebSocket base URL
  VITE_ADMIN_PASSWORD — Admin login password

CHAPTER FOUR: THE OCCUPANCY DETECTION SYSTEM
================================================================================

4.1 Overview
-------------
The occupancy detection module uses the YOLOv8 nano object detection model to
count the number of people present in a classroom from a single camera frame.
The result is stored in the classroom's database document and broadcast in
real time to all connected frontend clients. This provides administrators and
lecturers with continuous awareness of classroom headcount without any manual
counting or reporting.


4.2 Image Ingestion
--------------------
Camera devices are registered in the system with a unique deviceId. When a
device sends an image to the backend, it does so via a multipart HTTP POST
request to the endpoint:

  POST /classrooms/{classId}/image

The request must include the device's deviceId as a form field. The backend
validates this against the deviceId stored for the classroom, ensuring that
only the authorised camera for a given room can submit images. A mismatch
results in a 403 Forbidden response.

The image is received as an UploadFile object and its raw bytes are read into
memory. OpenCV's imdecode function decodes the bytes into a numpy array
(height × width × 3, BGR colour order). If decoding fails — indicating a
corrupt or unsupported file — the server returns a 400 Bad Request.


4.3 Model Inference
--------------------
The YOLOv8 nano model (yolov8n.pt) is loaded lazily: on the first image
request after server startup, the model is loaded and stored in
app.state.yolo_model. Subsequent requests reuse the cached model, avoiding
the multi-second load cost on every inference.

Model loading and inference are both executed in a thread pool executor to
prevent blocking the async event loop during CPU-intensive computation. The
inference call is:

  model.predict(img, imgsz=1920, conf=0.25, iou=0.45, augment=True)

Each parameter was selected with deliberate intent:

  imgsz=1920  — Processes the input at up to 1920 pixels on the longer edge.
                 A higher resolution allows the model to detect smaller or
                 more distant people who might be missed at 640px (the default).

  conf=0.25   — Only bounding boxes with a confidence score of at least 25%
                 are considered. This is lower than the default of 0.5,
                 accepting more detections at the risk of some false positives.
                 In a classroom, false positives (detecting a person where
                 there is none) are less harmful than false negatives (missing
                 a student who is present).

  iou=0.45    — Intersection-over-Union threshold for non-maximum suppression.
                 Overlapping boxes above this threshold are merged into one,
                 preventing the same person from being counted twice.

  augment=True — Test-time augmentation runs inference on several transformed
                  versions of the image (flipped, scaled) and merges the
                  results. This improves accuracy at the cost of roughly
                  doubling inference time.


4.4 Person Counting and Occupancy Calculation
----------------------------------------------
YOLO returns a set of detected bounding boxes, each annotated with a class
index corresponding to a category in the COCO dataset. Class index 0 in the
COCO dataset corresponds to the "person" class. The backend filters all
returned boxes by cls == 0 and counts the total.

The raw count is then capped at the room's registered capacity:

  occupancy = min(person_count, classroom.capacity)

This ensures that the displayed occupancy never exceeds the physical maximum
even if detection artefacts or overlapping people cause YOLO to return more
boxes than seats. The capacity ceiling also prevents misleading displays to
administrators who may be monitoring room safety.


4.5 Image Annotation and Storage
----------------------------------
After inference, the annotated image is produced by drawing two text overlays
onto the frame using OpenCV's putText function:

  "Occupancy: {count}/{capacity}"
  Current timestamp in the Africa/Lagos timezone (e.g. "26 Apr 2026, 10:45 AM")

The annotated frame is then JPEG-encoded back to bytes and uploaded to
Cloudinary under the folder "smart_classrooms/". The URL returned by Cloudinary
is stored in the classroom's latestImage field. The previous image URL is
simultaneously deleted from Cloudinary to prevent accumulation of orphaned files.

It is important to note that this annotated occupancy image is the only image
the system ever stores. Raw student registration photographs are never stored
anywhere — this is discussed in detail in Chapter Six.


4.6 Database Update and Real-time Broadcast
---------------------------------------------
After Cloudinary upload, the classroom document in MongoDB is updated:

  occupancy   ← new person count (capped at capacity)
  latestImage ← new Cloudinary URL
  updated_at  ← current server timestamp

The WebSocket broadcast manager then sends a "classroom_image_update" event
to all connected clients. The event payload contains the complete updated
classroom document. Every browser currently viewing the Dashboard page receives
this event and immediately updates the displayed occupancy count and live image
without any page refresh or polling.

The endpoint also returns a performance metrics object in its response body,
reporting the time taken for each stage of the pipeline in milliseconds
(decode, inference, upload, database write, broadcast). This supports
performance monitoring and helps diagnose bottlenecks during deployment.

CHAPTER FIVE: THE ATTENDANCE TRACKING SYSTEM
================================================================================

5.1 Overview
-------------
The attendance tracking system uses facial recognition to automatically identify
enrolled students from live classroom camera frames and record their presence
during an active session. The system is designed so that a student walking into
a classroom during an active session will be identified and marked present
within seconds of being captured in a camera frame, without any action required
from either the student or the lecturer.


5.2 Student Registration
-------------------------
Face recognition requires that each student's face embedding be stored in the
database before they can be identified during a session. This registration
process is initiated by the lecturer and completed by the student.

5.2.1 Course Registration Token

When a lecturer creates a course, the backend automatically generates a UUID
registration token and associates it with the course document. This token
serves as a one-time URL component:

  https://{frontend}/register/{token}

The lecturer shares this link with students — via a learning management system,
email, or displayed in class. The link is public and requires no login,
allowing any student to self-register without an institutional account.

5.2.2 Consent Collection

Before any personal data can be collected, the registration page presents three
mandatory consent declarations. Each must be explicitly checked before the form
can be submitted:

  Biometric consent — The student acknowledges that their photographs will be
  processed to create a facial recognition template for automated attendance.

  Manual alternative acknowledgment — The student confirms they have been
  informed that a manual attendance alternative exists and that not consenting
  carries no academic penalty.

  Age declaration — The student confirms they are 18 years of age or older,
  or have obtained parental or guardian consent to participate.

These consents are validated server-side. The backend endpoint returns a 422
Unprocessable Entity error if any of the three consent flags is not explicitly
set to "true" in the submitted form data. Trusting only the client-side
checkbox validation would allow a technically capable user to bypass consent
requirements by calling the API directly.

5.2.3 Photo Upload and Embedding Extraction

The student uploads between one and five clear, front-facing photographs. The
frontend provides a drag-and-drop interface with image previews and counts the
uploaded files against the maximum. Variety in angle and lighting across
multiple photos improves recognition accuracy, as the system stores all
embeddings and checks the current frame against all of them.

On submission, the frontend sends a multipart/form-data POST request to:

  POST /courses/{courseCode}/register

containing the matric number, full name, consent flags, and image files.

For each photograph, the backend performs the following operations:

  a. The image bytes are loaded using face_recognition.load_image_file(),
     which uses Pillow (PIL) internally and always produces an RGB-ordered
     numpy array regardless of the original image format. This is the correct
     colour order expected by the dlib face encoder.

  b. face_recognition.face_encodings(rgb) is called. This function performs
     three operations internally:
       - Detects all face bounding boxes in the image using dlib's Histogram
         of Oriented Gradients (HOG) face detector.
       - Runs the 68-point facial landmark predictor on each detected face
         to identify the positions of eyes, nose, mouth, and jaw.
       - Extracts the face chip (aligned face region) and passes it through
         a ResNet-based face encoder to produce a 128-dimensional float vector.

  c. If no face is detected in a given photo, the backend returns a 422 error
     specifying which photo failed. The student is asked to re-upload a
     clearer image.

  d. If a face is successfully detected and encoded, the 128-float vector is
     added to the student's embeddings list. The image bytes are then
     discarded. They are not written to disk, not stored in the database,
     and not uploaded to any cloud service.

At the end of processing, the student document is created or updated in MongoDB
with the full list of embedding vectors and all consent metadata. An Enrollment
document linking the student to the course is also created.

5.2.4 Data Minimisation (NDPA s.24)

The architectural decision to discard photographs immediately after encoding
is a deliberate and non-negotiable implementation of the data minimisation
principle under Section 24 of the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023. The
registered data — 128-dimensional float vectors — cannot be reverse-engineered
into a photograph of the student's face. The transformation is mathematically
one-way: an embedding can identify a face but cannot reconstruct it.


5.3 Session Management
-----------------------
Attendance tracking operates within the context of a Session. A session
represents one instance of a course meeting in a specific classroom.

A lecturer starts a session by submitting a POST request to /sessions with the
courseCode and classId. The backend enforces a constraint that only one session
may be active per classroom at a time — attempting to start a second session
in an occupied classroom returns a 409 Conflict error. When a session is
started, a "session_started" WebSocket event is broadcast to all connected
clients.

The session remains active until the lecturer explicitly ends it via
POST /sessions/{sessionId}/end. Once ended, the status is set to "ended" and
an endedAt timestamp is recorded. Face recognition will no longer mark
attendance for an ended session.


5.4 Automatic Attendance Marking
----------------------------------
Automatic attendance marking is triggered by the same image endpoint that
handles occupancy detection:

  POST /classrooms/{classId}/image

After YOLO completes its person count, the backend queries the database for
an active session associated with this classId. If no active session is found,
the face recognition pipeline is skipped entirely and the endpoint returns
immediately after updating occupancy. This design ensures that face recognition
compute is only expended during actual academic sessions.

When an active session is found, the following pipeline executes:

5.4.1 Enrolled Student Retrieval

The backend fetches the list of matric numbers enrolled in the session's course
and passes them to a specialised database query:

  database.get_students_for_recognition(matric_numbers)

This query filters the enrolled students to return only those for whom:
  - embeddingsDeleted is not True (student has not withdrawn consent)
  - embeddings array is non-empty (student has completed registration)

Students who withdrew consent or did not register are automatically excluded
from the recognition pipeline without any additional logic.

5.4.2 Face Detection and Embedding Extraction

face_recognition.face_locations(rgb) detects the bounding boxes of all faces
present in the current frame. face_recognition.face_encodings(rgb, locations)
extracts a 128-float embedding for each detected face. If the frame contains
no faces, the pipeline exits immediately.

5.4.3 Distance-Based Matching

For each face detected in the current frame, the backend computes the Euclidean
distance between the detected embedding and every stored embedding for every
enrolled student:

  distances = face_recognition.face_distance(student.embeddings, detected_embedding)
  best_distance = min(distances)

A student may have up to five stored embeddings (one per registration photo).
The system checks against all of them and uses the best (lowest) distance.
This means that even if one registration photo was of poor quality, a good
match on another photo will still result in a correct identification.

The match is confirmed if:

  best_distance < 0.55

This threshold was chosen to balance precision and recall in a controlled
classroom environment. The Euclidean distance ranges from 0 (identical faces)
to values above 1 (completely different faces). A threshold of 0.55 is more
conservative than the library's default recommendation of 0.6, reducing the
risk of false identifications in settings where consequential academic records
are being produced.

5.4.4 Attendance Record Update

When a match is confirmed, database.mark_attendance() is called with the
session ID, the matched matric number, and method="auto". This function is
designed to be idempotent — safe to call repeatedly for the same student:

  - If the student already has an attendance record that was not manually
    overridden: the markedAt timestamp is updated to reflect the most recent
    detection. The student is not duplicated.

  - If the student has a manually overridden record (placed by the lecturer):
    the function does nothing. The lecturer's explicit decision takes precedence
    over the automatic system and cannot be overwritten by subsequent camera
    frames.

  - If the student has no existing record: a new AttendanceEntry is inserted
    into the session's attendees array with method="auto".

5.4.5 Real-time Broadcast

If any students were newly marked in the current frame, a "attendance_update"
WebSocket event is broadcast to all connected clients. The event payload includes
the full updated session document and the list of newly identified matric
numbers. The lecturer's browser reflects the updated attendance list without
any manual refresh.


5.5 Manual Attendance Override
--------------------------------
The system acknowledges that face recognition is not infallible. A student
may be missed if they are seated at an angle the camera cannot capture, if
lighting conditions are poor, or if their registration photos were not
representative of their current appearance.

Lecturers are provided with a manual override interface that allows them to
explicitly mark any enrolled student as present or absent at any time during
or after a session:

  PUT /sessions/{sessionId}/attendance/{matricNumber}
  Body: { status: "present" | "absent" }

When this endpoint is called:
  - The attendance entry is flagged with manuallyOverridden: True.
  - If the status is "present", the entry is upserted with method="manual".
  - If the status is "absent", the entry is removed from the attendees array
    (absence is represented by the absence of an entry, not a flag, consistent
    with the automatic pipeline behaviour).
  - Once manuallyOverridden is True, subsequent automatic detections cannot
    modify that student's record for the remainder of the session.

The final attendance report for any session therefore reflects either automatic
face recognition results or lecturer-corrected results, with the
manuallyOverridden flag preserved in the record for audit purposes.


================================================================================
CHAPTER SIX: INTEGRATION OF OCCUPANCY AND ATTENDANCE
================================================================================

6.1 Shared Entry Point
------------------------
The occupancy detection and attendance tracking pipelines are not separate
systems — they are two stages of a single image processing pipeline triggered
by one HTTP request. The endpoint POST /classrooms/{classId}/image serves as
the unified entry point for both functions.

When a camera device sends an image, the backend executes the following
sequential pipeline:

  Stage 1: Image decoding (OpenCV)
  Stage 2: YOLO inference → person count → update classroom occupancy
  Stage 3: Check for active session
    If no session → end pipeline, return occupancy result
    If session found → continue to Stage 4
  Stage 4: Face recognition → identify students → mark attendance
  Stage 5: Annotate image with occupancy and session info
  Stage 6: Upload annotated image to Cloudinary
  Stage 7: Update classroom document in MongoDB
  Stage 8: Broadcast WebSocket events


6.2 Shared Classroom Anchor
-----------------------------
Both systems use the classId as their shared reference point. YOLO updates the
occupancy of the classroom identified by classId. The face recognition pipeline
fetches the active session for the same classId. Both pipelines therefore
operate on the same physical space without explicit coupling — they are
independently implemented functions that share only the classId lookup context.


6.3 Independent Operation
---------------------------
Occupancy detection operates continuously regardless of whether attendance
tracking is active. A classroom can monitor headcount at all hours without any
course or session configured. Attendance tracking, by contrast, operates only
when a session is explicitly started by a lecturer. This design ensures that
face recognition is not running — and students are not being identified — outside
of formal academic sessions, which has important implications for consent and
data protection.

The two systems also fail independently. If face_recognition or dlib is not
installed on the deployment server, the backend logs a warning and skips the
face recognition stage. Occupancy detection continues to function normally.
This allows the system to be deployed in a lightweight "occupancy-only" mode
on servers without the computational resources or dependencies required for
face recognition.


6.4 Combined Real-time Output
------------------------------
The WebSocket broadcast layer unifies the real-time output of both pipelines
to all connected clients. The frontend Dashboard page listens for
"classroom_image_update" events to update occupancy counts and live images.
The CourseDetail page listens for "attendance_update" events to update the
session attendance list. Both event types flow through the same WebSocket
connection, so the lecturer sees a unified real-time view of their classroom's
occupancy and attendance without managing multiple connections.


================================================================================
CHAPTER SEVEN: NDPA COMPLIANCE AND CONSENT MANAGEMENT
================================================================================

7.1 Data Minimisation (NDPA s.24)
-----------------------------------
Section 24 of the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 requires that personal data
collected must be adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary in
relation to the purposes for which it is processed.

In the context of this system, the necessary data for automated attendance is
the ability to recognise a student's face — not the photograph itself. The
face_recognition library produces a 128-dimensional float vector (the embedding)
that captures the mathematical relationships between facial features in a form
sufficient for identity verification. This vector cannot be used to reconstruct
or render the original face.

The system therefore discards all uploaded photographs immediately after the
embedding extraction step. No photograph is written to disk, stored in the
database, or uploaded to cloud storage. The only biometric artifact retained is
the embedding vector, which represents the minimum data necessary to fulfil the
system's purpose.


7.2 Right of Access (NDPA s.34)
---------------------------------
Section 34 establishes a data subject's right to obtain confirmation of whether
personal data concerning them is being processed and to access that data.

The system implements this through the student portal endpoint:

  GET /students/lookup/{matricNumber}

This public endpoint requires no login. Any student can enter their matric
number and receive a complete summary of all data held about them, including:
  - All courses they are enrolled in and their attendance rate per course
  - A session-by-session breakdown of their attendance with dates, classroom
    names, status (present/absent), and whether each record was manually
    overridden
  - Their consent record: the date consent was given, the consent version,
    whether biometrics are currently active, and the date consent was withdrawn
    if applicable

No data about the student is withheld from this view.


7.3 Right to Erasure (NDPA s.36)
----------------------------------
Section 36 establishes the right of a data subject to have their personal data
erased without undue delay under certain circumstances, including withdrawal of
consent.

The system implements consent withdrawal through:

  DELETE /courses/{courseCode}/students/biometrics
  Body: { matricNumber: str }

When this endpoint is called, the backend:
  - Sets the student's embeddings array to empty
  - Sets embeddingsDeleted: True
  - Records the consentWithdrawnAt timestamp

From this point forward, the student is automatically excluded from all face
recognition queries. The embeddingsDeleted flag is checked in the database
query that fetches students for recognition — no code change or system restart
is required for the exclusion to take effect.

The student's non-biometric records are preserved: their matric number, name,
enrollment history, and all past attendance records remain intact. This is
consistent with the principle that the right to erasure applies to the personal
data that was the subject of consent (the biometric template), not to the
administrative records of academic participation.


7.4 Consent Version Tracking
------------------------------
The system records a consentVersion field alongside the consentTimestamp.
This supports future scenarios where the consent terms are revised — for
example, if the system is updated to process data in a new way that requires
updated consent. The version field allows the institution to identify students
who consented under earlier terms and request renewed consent if required.

8.2 Bugs Identified and Fixed During Testing
---------------------------------------------
The following defects were discovered during systematic API testing:

BUG 1 — DELETE /courses/{courseCode}/students/biometrics returning 422
  The route handler was using the ManualAttendanceRequest schema, which requires
  both a matricNumber and a present boolean field. The biometrics deletion
  endpoint only needs a matricNumber. FastAPI's request validation therefore
  rejected every call to this endpoint with a 422 error.

  Fix: A dedicated DeleteBiometricsRequest schema was added to schemas.py
  containing only the matricNumber field. The route handler was updated to use
  this schema.

BUG 2 — Registration form sending "[object Object]" as error message
  FastAPI validation errors (HTTP 422) return the detail field as an array of
  objects rather than a string. The API client's error handler performed
  new Error(array), which JavaScript converts to the string "[object Object]".

  Fix: The throwOnError helper was updated to check the type of the detail
  field. If it is an array, the .msg property of each element is extracted and
  joined into a human-readable string. If it is a string, it is used directly.

BUG 3 — Session cards showing "1 / 0 present" instead of enrolled count
  The transformSession function hardcoded totalStudents: 0 because the backend
  session object does not include enrollment count. This caused the session
  card to always display the enrolled count as zero.

  Fix: After loading both session and enrollment data, the frontend now sets
  totalStudents from the length of the loaded students array.

BUG 4 — Registration failing with "Unsupported image type" from dlib
  The dlib pre-built wheel for Windows (dlib-19.24.1-cp311-cp311-win_amd64)
  was compiled against NumPy 1.x. The installed NumPy version was 2.4.4.
  NumPy 2.0 changed its internal C API structure, causing dlib to misread
  array metadata and reject all images — including synthetic test images —
  with the above error.

  Fix: NumPy was downgraded to 1.26.4, the final NumPy 1.x release for
  Python 3.11. Both dlib and OpenCV were confirmed working after the downgrade.
  This issue is specific to the local Windows development environment. On Linux
  deployment platforms, dlib is compiled from source against the installed NumPy
  version, so ABI compatibility is guaranteed automatically.


8.3 Face Recognition Testing

  - The student was enrolled in the test course.

CHAPTER NINE: CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
================================================================================

9.1 Conclusion
---------------
This project has successfully designed and implemented a smart classroom system
that unifies real-time occupancy detection and biometric attendance tracking in
a single, privacy-aware platform. The system demonstrates that automated
attendance marking using face recognition can be made practical, legally
compliant, and operationally robust in an institutional context.

The dual-pipeline architecture — where YOLO and face recognition both operate
from the same camera frame sent by a single device — maximises the utility of
each image submission without requiring additional hardware or network calls.
The WebSocket broadcast layer ensures that occupancy and attendance data reach
the administrative frontend in real time, providing lecturers and administrators
with continuous awareness of their classrooms.

The NDPA-compliant consent framework, data minimisation implementation, and
right-to-erasure endpoint demonstrate that biometric systems can be built to
respect data subject rights by design rather than as an afterthought. The
architectural decision to store only embedding vectors — and never photographs
— represents a meaningful and enforceable privacy guarantee.


9.2 Limitations
----------------
  i.   The face recognition matching threshold (0.55) is a fixed value. In
       environments with challenging lighting or higher student counts, this
       may require tuning on a per-deployment basis.

  ii.  The system relies on the YOLOv8 nano model for occupancy, which trades
       accuracy for speed. Overcrowded or poorly lit classrooms may yield
       inaccurate counts.

  iii. Admin authentication is implemented client-side without a server-side
       session or token. This is not suitable for public-facing deployment
       without replacement by a proper authentication system.

  iv.  The student portal returns a 404 error if a student has no ended sessions,
       rather than an empty results set. This may cause confusion for newly
       registered students with no session history.


9.3 Recommendations for Future Work
-------------------------------------
  i.   Implement server-side authentication using JWT tokens or OAuth 2.0 to
       protect administrative endpoints from unauthorised access.

  ii.  Introduce an adaptive threshold mechanism that adjusts the face matching
       threshold based on the confidence distribution of matches observed over
       time for a given deployment environment.

  iii. Add liveness detection to the face recognition pipeline to prevent
       spoofing attacks where a photograph of a student is held up in front of
       the camera.

  iv.  Extend the analytics module to provide aggregate attendance trends,
       at-risk student identification (students below the 75% threshold), and
       exportable reports in PDF and Excel formats for submission to academic
       boards.

  v.   Implement a lecturer mobile application to allow session management and
       attendance monitoring on a smartphone rather than requiring a laptop.

  vi.  Replace the hardcoded matching threshold with a configurable per-course
       setting, allowing different courses to apply different sensitivity levels
       based on their enrolment size and classroom layout.
